Nigeria Uyo Mission — Closing Discussion

Do Not Look Beyond the Mark

A discussion for you — by direction of President Richard Paapa Dadzie
President Dadzie's Direction: This closing discussion would be included in every training gathering. It is not only about how we teach — it is about how we live, how we think, how we carry ourselves, and how we represent Jesus Christ in every interaction we have as His disciples.
"But behold, the Jews were a stiffnecked people; and they despised the words of plainness, and killed the prophets, and sought for things that they could not understand. Wherefore, because of their blindness, which blindness came by looking beyond the mark, they must needs fall."
Jacob 4:14

The Mark Is Christ

Jacob's warning was not given to wicked people. It was given to people who believed in God — people who read scripture, who prayed, who considered themselves spiritually serious. Yet they fell. Not because they stopped looking, but because they looked in the wrong direction. They looked past the simplicity of Christ — the mark — and sought for something more complicated, more impressive, or more self-determined. And in doing so, they stumbled.

The mark, in every dispensation, has always been the same: Jesus Christ. His gospel. His example. His word. Everything a missionary needs — for teaching, for living, for making decisions, for interacting with others — is found within that mark. The moment we step outside it, even with good intentions, we put ourselves in danger.

"Look unto me in every thought; doubt not, fear not." D&C 6:36

This Is Bigger Than Teaching

Looking beyond the mark is not only a teaching problem. It is a life problem. It shows up in how a missionary thinks, learns, and conducts themselves every single day of their mission.

It shows up in how you learn.
A missionary who looks beyond the mark is not satisfied learning what has been given. They chase after deeper mysteries, speculative ideas, or the opinions of others — rather than rooting themselves in the plain and precious truths of the restored gospel. Intelligence is a gift from God. But intelligence that is not anchored in the Spirit and in revealed truth leads us away from the mark, not toward it.
It shows up in how you observe others.
In the mission field, you will encounter many kinds of missionaries. Some will inspire you. Some will disappoint you. A missionary who looks beyond the mark learns their habits, their shortcuts, and their compromises from those around them — especially from poor examples — and calls it experience. A true disciple of Christ does not learn discipleship from whoever is nearest. They learn it from Christ.
It shows up in how you handle what you don't understand.
Every missionary will encounter doctrinal questions, life situations, and personal struggles they cannot fully explain. A missionary who looks beyond the mark reaches for speculation, for human wisdom, or for the philosophies of the world to fill the gap. A wise missionary says what Nephi said: "I know that he loveth his children; nevertheless I do not know the meaning of all things." Humility before God is never weakness. It is the mark.
It shows up in how you present yourself.
Looking beyond the mark can also mean looking beyond your calling — trying to be more than a representative of Jesus Christ. The missionary who performs, impresses, or seeks recognition has looked past the mark. The missionary who simply and purely serves, teaches, and loves has stayed exactly where God needs them.
"For I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified." 1 Corinthians 2:2

Learn From Christ, Not From the Field

One of the most subtle dangers of mission life is this: you will be surrounded by fellow missionaries for two years, and without realising it, you will begin to learn what a missionary looks like from those around you — rather than from Christ Himself.

Some missionaries cut corners on study. Some are unkind to their companions. Some are prideful in how they teach. Some are casual about the sacred covenants they made. If you are not careful, these patterns begin to look normal — and you absorb them not by choosing to, but simply by being near them long enough.

God did not send you to become what the field made you. He sent you to become what He intended when He called you. Stay close to that original calling. Return to it every day in your personal study, your prayer, and your character.

"Come unto Christ, and be perfected in him, and deny yourselves of all ungodliness." Moroni 10:32
"Be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity." 1 Timothy 4:12

What Church Leaders Have Said

"There is a beauty and clarity that comes from simplicity that we sometimes do not appreciate in our thirst for intricate solutions." — Elder Dieter F. Uchtdorf, "Of Things That Matter Most," General Conference, October 2010
"Some people seem to be embarrassed by the simplicity of the Savior's message. … We look beyond the mark when we refuse to accept simple gospel truths for what they are." — Elder Quentin L. Cook, "Looking beyond the Mark," Ensign, March 2003
"They must have reveled in speculative and theoretical matters that obscured for them the fundamental spiritual truths." — Elder Dean L. Larsen, "Looking beyond the Mark," General Conference, October 1987
"He will teach in simplicity, according to the true needs of the people, basic gospel doctrines like faith, repentance, and prayer, which all men — all men — can apply. He will not look beyond the mark by exaggerating, by teaching in the fringe areas, by expanding on the scriptures, or by teaching exotic extremes in any principle…" — Elder Gene R. Cook, "Spiritual Guides for Teachers of Righteousness," General Conference, April 1982
"These few individuals let their minds seek to run far ahead of their confirming behavior. For them, exciting exploration is preferred to plodding implementation. Speculation and argumentation are more fun than consecration for these individuals." — Elder Neal A. Maxwell, "The Inexhaustible Gospel," BYU devotional address, August 18, 1992

Three Questions to Ask Yourself Daily

1. Am I learning from Christ — or from whoever is nearest?
Your study, your habits, and your character should reflect the Savior — not the strongest personality in your zone or district.
2. Am I seeking what has been revealed — or what I wish were revealed?
God has given us everything we need. Contentment with His word is not spiritual laziness. It is faith.
3. Am I standing as a true disciple — or performing for an audience?
The mark is not how others see you. The mark is who you are when no one is watching.
"Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths." Proverbs 3:5-6

Closing Discussion Questions

Discussion 1
Have you noticed yourself picking up habits or attitudes from missionaries around you — rather than from the Savior? What were they, and what do you want to do about it?
Discussion 2
Where in your daily life — not just your teaching — are you most tempted to look beyond the mark? What pulls you there?
Discussion 3
What would it look like for you, specifically, to stay with the mark this week — in your study, your interactions, your companionship, and your character?
"The mark is not a destination you arrive at once.
It is a direction you choose every single day.
And the mark has always been, and will always be, Jesus Christ."
A final word for you:

You were not sent here to figure out your own version of discipleship. You were sent here to find Christ — and then to become what finding Him does to a person. That is the mark. Stay there. Come back to it when you drift. Teach from it. Live from it. And when you go home, take it with you for the rest of your life.
